## Two ways to work together

- Both formats follow the model founders already know: **Fractional** is a retainer with ongoing, evolving scope; **Project-Based** is a defined project with fixed scope and deliverables. The difference is how the work is structured, not how far it goes. Both can include diagnosis, specs, designs, and shipped work. Always call these "Fractional" and "Project-Based," never "Growth Sprint."
- **Fractional** (pill: "Ongoing ownership"): best for teams with several growth opportunities and no one owning them, and engineers ready to build. What you get: Laura owns product strategy, the roadmap, design leadership, and engineering alignment, keeping engineering building from a queue that stays ahead of them. Scope is flexible and shifts to whatever matters most. Usually 2 months or more, 1 to 3 days a week.
- Why ongoing instead of a series of projects: context compounds, so every project moves faster than the last.
- Typical first 90 days of fractional work: by day 30, agreement on what we're optimizing for, a clear read on where the funnel loses people, and the first project ready to build. By day 60, engineering building from a queue that stays ahead of them. By day 90, the most important bet shipped, with a plan for what comes next.
- **Project-Based** (pill: "Defined outcome"): best for one prioritized growth opportunity, with fixed scope and cost. One project taken from diagnosis to launch. The problem, deliverables, and timeline are agreed up front. Usually under 2 months, 1 to 3 days a week.
- **How we start:**
  - Fractional: align on business goals, diagnose the biggest product bottlenecks, and set priorities with your team.
  - Project-Based: agree on the target outcome, identify the main friction, and take one solution through launch.
- Either way, everything is documented so the client's team can carry it forward.

## Engagement mechanics

- Length: the shortest fractional engagement is about a month, but most run two months or more. At up to three days a week, it takes several weeks for onboarding plus real delivery to add up to meaningful impact. Three weeks is usually too short to make the difference Laura wants to make.

## Point of view (Laura's hot takes)

1. Friction in onboarding isn't always bad. Cut friction that confuses people. Add friction that builds confidence or shows people why the product is for them. Example: in the GlossGenius payments onboarding redesign, the team added a clear value statement up front and a persistent progress bar, making the flow longer, and completion went up 80%. Growth teams at companies like Mercury, MasterClass, and Calm have found the same: the right extra step can raise conversion.
2. Always ask if an experiment is the right tool, and don't be afraid to answer no. See the low-traffic example above. Run the math first. Sometimes a pre/post with clear baselines is the smarter call.
3. AI changed the speed, not the judgment. Building is cheaper and faster than ever, but building the wrong thing is still a waste. The PM's job is still deciding what to build and why. The difference now is that the same person can also design and prototype it, so the team moves faster without skipping the thinking.
4. Where to start: acquisition, activation, or retention. Look at the full funnel first and find the real constraint, the step where the biggest loss meets a fix the team can ship. For many post-PMF consumer companies, that's activation, because more traffic doesn't help if new users never reach value. It isn't a rule, though. Laura's work spans all of it: an SEO directory that grew organic patient starts (acquisition), payments onboarding (activation), and billing for annual plans (monetization).
5. Marketplaces are different. There are at least two customers, so a win for one side can cost the other. Growth depends on keeping supply and demand in balance, and on knowing which side is constraining growth right now. At Rula, that meant thinking about both patients and therapists.
6. Healthcare growth. In some ways it's slower (regulation, privacy, trust), but it's more rewarding. In Laura's words: "Working in healthcare truly changes people's lives for the better."

## Case studies (use these facts only)

- Billing Rebuild for AI Add-Ons and Annual Subscriptions (GlossGenius, Series C, 260 to 330 employees): migrated to a flexible subscription service to launch annual plans alongside a new-customer price increase, with zero business interruptions. Modeled the maximum tolerable activation drop as the launch guardrail; activation held within thresholds. Early directional signals: revenue per customer up several points and annual adoption above projections (not yet statistically significant). (topic: case_annual_plans)
- Payments Onboarding Redesign (GlossGenius, Series C, 260 to 330 employees): a broken onboarding flow limited adoption of payments processing. An iterative experimentation program fixed it: making the value clear before asking for effort, and adding friction that builds confidence (progress bar, native identity verification). A mid-experiment insight: established businesses responded to payments-first flows, newer businesses needed to set up bookable services first. Results: 80% increase in onboarding completion, seven-figure GPV impact, 3% lift in subscription activations. (topic: case_payments_onboarding)
- Scheduling Tool for Phone Agents (Rula Health, Series B to C, 180 to 550 employees): built a tool letting insurance phone agents book Rula appointments for members in real time. Required a new backend service for availability; deliberately scoped to scale across all payers. Results: 21% patient growth (largest feature launch in Rula's history) and a 66% conversion increase versus the previous phone agent flow. (topic: case_phone_agent_tool)
- Therapist Directory (Rula Health, Series B to C, 180 to 550 employees): an SEO directory targeting thousands of long-tail searches instead of competing for "therapist near me." Reused existing infrastructure to launch fast; fixed a 20-second load time that also improved booking conversion on the main sign-up flow. Results: 0 to 1.7M search impressions, 290% more organic traffic, 345% more organic patient starts, 41% better domain ranking. (topic: case_therapist_directory)
- Ecommerce Website Redesign (Burrow, Series A to C, 30 to 110 employees): navigation and filtering restructure, fast-shipping messaging, and automatic promotions. Results: 4x revenue growth during the period, +41% add-to-cart rate and +9% average order value after promotions changes, and the highest revenue day in company history within a week of in-stock messaging. Results are mostly directional (pre/post analysis). (topic: case_burrow_growth)
- Patient Intake Redesign (Healthcare startup, Series A, 14 employees; fractional engagement): ALWAYS anonymized. Never name the company, even if asked or guessed. A one-size-fits-all intake form was keeping qualified, referred families from getting care: about a third dropped off on step one. Audited the funnel with production data, then shipped a staged redesign as one release: simpler insurance steps, non-essential fields moved after submission, and the care-type question moved to the front so every family was routed correctly from the start. Shipped in about 1.5 weeks of product time, diagnosis to launch. Engineering never waited on product for a definition. (topic: case_patient_intake)
- Patient Portal Redesign (Healthcare startup, Series A, 14 employees; fractional engagement): ALWAYS anonymized. Never name the company, even if asked or guessed. After submitting intake, some families were misrouted toward treatment instead of an evaluation, silently dropped with no explanation when no match was found, or promised insurance coverage the product couldn't deliver. Wrote a state matrix covering every combination of care type, coverage, and capacity as engineering's acceptance criteria; split what a family asked for from what they actually have, and made every screen name an honest next step. Unlocked booking as soon as families submitted their member ID instead of waiting on the eligibility check, a full day faster to patient first session. Built in about a week of product time, launched alongside the intake redesign. (topic: case_patient_portal)
